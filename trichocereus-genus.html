Trichocereus / Echinopsis

Kew treats Trichocereus as a synonym of the broader genus Echinopsis; Leucostele is a separate accepted genus for part of this group, including terscheckii.

Quick facts

RankGenus
Family / Subfamily / TribeCactaceae / Cactoideae / Trichocereinae (subtribe of Cereeae), per the 2025 phylogenomic classification used elsewhere on this site; historically the broader tribe Trichocereeae (Hunt et al., The New Cactus Lexicon, 2006)
Accepted genusEchinopsis Zucc. — a broad (sensu lato) circumscription that includes the former genus Trichocereus as a synonym. As of an October 2025 POWO update, the same broad Echinopsis also includes Soehrensia, Chamaecereus and Lobivia as synonyms (Wikipedia).
Type speciesEchinopsis eyriesii, now treated as Echinopsis oxygona (Wikipedia)
Accepted species (genus-wide)About 80, per Plants of the World Online as reported by Wikipedia (November 2025). Only the species this Codex has separately verified are listed below.
Genus-rank synonymTrichocereus (A.Berger) Riccob., Boll. Reale Orto Bot. Palermo 8: 236 (1909) — a synonym of Echinopsis under Kew's primary treatment; accepted as its own genus under WCVP's Govaerts et al. (2021) "alternative taxonomy."
Separate accepted genusLeucostele Backeb. emend. Schlumpb., Cactaceae Syst. Init. 28: 29 (2012) — erected on the strength of Schlumpberger & Renner's 2012 molecular phylogeny, which found Echinopsis sensu lato (as then circumscribed) to be polyphyletic. Covers the Chilean, northwestern-Argentine and Bolivian "white-spined" Trichocereus-type columnars, including terscheckii.

Description

Three names cover this generic complex, and current authorities do not fully agree on how to divide it. Kew's primary treatment accepts one broad genus, Echinopsis, and places Trichocereus as a synonym of it; the same Kew dataset separately documents WCVP's "alternative taxonomy," which instead keeps Trichocereus as its own genus. A third genus, Leucostele, was split off in 2012 on molecular evidence and is accepted by Kew as distinct from Echinopsis regardless of which treatment of Trichocereus is followed; it covers the terscheckii/atacamensis-type columnars rather than the pachanoi/peruvianus/bridgesii group. A genus label by itself is not evidence of a particular clone's parentage — see each species page for its own synonymy and horticultural-name history.

Distribution

Collectively, this generic complex spans the Andes from southern Ecuador and Peru (pachanoi) through Bolivia and Peru (macrogona) and Bolivia (lageniformis) to Bolivia and northwestern Argentina (Leucostele terscheckii).
